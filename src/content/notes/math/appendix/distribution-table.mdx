### Distributions

| Distribution | Support | Mass or density | Mean | Variance | When it fits | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bernoulli$(p)$ | $\{0, 1\}$ | $p^x(1-p)^{1-x}$ | $p$ | $p(1-p)$ | one yes/no trial | [Common distributions](/workbench/math/probability/common-distributions/) |
| Binomial$(n, p)$ | $\{0, \dots, n\}$ | $\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ | $np$ | $np(1-p)$ | successes in $n$ independent trials | [Common distributions](/workbench/math/probability/common-distributions/) |
| Geometric$(p)$ | $\{1, 2, \dots\}$ | $(1-p)^{k-1}p$ | $\frac{1}{p}$ | $\frac{1-p}{p^2}$ | trials until the first success | [Expectation and variance](/workbench/math/probability/expectation-and-variance/) |
| Poisson$(\lambda)$ | $\{0, 1, \dots\}$ | $e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}$ | $\lambda$ | $\lambda$ | events in a window at a steady rate | [Common distributions](/workbench/math/probability/common-distributions/) |
| Uniform$(a, b)$ | $[a, b]$ | $\frac{1}{b-a}$ | $\frac{a+b}{2}$ | $\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$ | every value in an interval equally likely | [Common distributions](/workbench/math/probability/common-distributions/) |
| Exponential$(\lambda)$ | $[0, \infty)$ | $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$ | $\frac{1}{\lambda}$ | $\frac{1}{\lambda^2}$ | waiting time with no memory | [Common distributions](/workbench/math/probability/common-distributions/) |
| Normal$(\mu, \sigma^2)$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ | $\mu$ | $\sigma^2$ | sums and averages of many small effects | [Central limit theorem](/workbench/math/probability/central-limit-theorem/) |

## Useful facts

- About $68\%$ of a normal lies within one standard deviation of its mean, about $95\%$ within two, and about $99.7\%$ within three.
- The Poisson is the limit of the binomial when $n$ is large, $p$ is small, and $np = \lambda$.
- The exponential is the only continuous distribution with no memory: $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$.
- A sum of independent normals is normal, with means and variances added. A sum of independent Poissons is Poisson with rates added.

<Callout type="pitfall">
Do not read a density value as a probability. For the exponential with rate $5$, the density at $0$ is $5$, which is larger than $1$ and still legitimate; only areas under the density are probabilities.
</Callout>
